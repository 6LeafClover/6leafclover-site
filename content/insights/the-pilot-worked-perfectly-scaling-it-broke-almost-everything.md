# The Pilot

Trentbridge Logistics ran a genuinely well-executed automation pilot on inbound goods processing at a single warehouse. It hit every target: on time, on budget, clean exception rates. The natural next step, and the one the business took, was to roll the same configuration out to twelve additional sites simultaneously, confident the pilot had proven the concept.

# What Went Wrong

Within weeks of the wider rollout, roughly half the new sites were generating exception rates the pilot site had never come close to. Investigation revealed the pilot's success had rested on conditions specific to that one location, invisible until they were removed: a particular data format used by one dominant local supplier group, and a manual pre-processing step one experienced team member performed as a matter of habit, without it ever being written into the documented process.

The pilot hadn't proven the automation worked in general. It had proven it worked in one specific, quietly non-representative context.

# The Rebuild

Rather than abandoning the twelve-site rollout, we restructured how scaling was approached:

- **A deliberate second-stage pilot** across three additional sites, chosen specifically because their operating conditions differed from the original pilot site and from each other
- **Structured variation mapping**, documenting every difference in supplier data, local process and system configuration across the second-stage sites before building anything further
- **A formal pilot-to-production bridge**, requiring evidence the automation handled genuine variation before the remaining nine sites were committed to

# The Result

The revised approach took roughly eight weeks longer than the original all-at-once plan. When the full twelve-site rollout did proceed, it launched with a fraction of the exceptions that had derailed the first attempt, because the variation that had caused them had already been found and designed for.

# The Lesson

A successful pilot proves an idea can work once, under one set of conditions. Whether it generalises is a genuinely different question, and one worth answering deliberately — with a small, varied second stage — before committing a full rollout budget to finding out the hard way.
