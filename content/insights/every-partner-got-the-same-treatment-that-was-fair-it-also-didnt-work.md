# The Situation

Kashmir Data Solutions had 90 partners and one operating model for all of them. Every partner received the same onboarding pack, the same quarterly check-in call, and access to the same marketing development fund, regardless of whether they were generating seven figures of annual revenue or hadn't closed a deal since they signed.

It felt like the right thing to do. Nobody wanted to be seen playing favourites. But spreading a fixed amount of partner management time evenly across 90 relationships of wildly different value meant the strongest partners were chronically under-served, while significant time went into partners who were never going to produce meaningful revenue.

# Building the Framework

We ran a segmentation exercise across three dimensions:

- **Current revenue contribution** — what the partner is actually delivering today
- **Growth potential** — market position, capability and appetite to invest
- **Strategic fit** — how well the partner's business aligns with where the vendor wants to go

This produced three tiers, each with a genuinely different model:

**Strategic partners** (a small group) received quarterly business reviews, co-investment in joint marketing, and named account management.

**Growth partners** received structured enablement, a defined path to the strategic tier, and regular but lighter-touch support.

**Registered partners** moved to a largely self-serve model — good documentation, an accessible portal, and support on request — freeing account management time that had previously been spread too thin to matter.

# The Result

Twelve months in, revenue from the strategic tier was up 40%. Just as importantly, the account team could point to exactly where their time was going and why, rather than dividing attention evenly across a list that didn't deserve it evenly.

# The Lesson

Fairness in a partner programme isn't about identical treatment. It's about giving each relationship the investment its value and potential have actually earned. A tiered model isn't unfair to smaller partners — it's honest with everyone about where they stand and what it takes to move up.
