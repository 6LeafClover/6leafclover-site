# The problem

Tangerine Manufacturing's automation team had put together a genuinely strong proposal: sound architecture, a credible vendor shortlist, a sensible phased rollout. It went to the board and stalled — not because the technology was in question, but because the projected return on investment rested on a single figure, "40% efficiency gain," that nobody in the room could trace back to how it had actually been calculated.

The CFO's question was simple and entirely fair: where does that number come from? Nobody in the meeting could answer it with confidence.

# Rebuilding from the right direction

The original business case had been built by the technology team, working forward from what the platform could theoretically do. We rebuilt it working backward from finance instead:

- **Specific target processes**, named individually rather than described as a category — invoice matching, purchase order reconciliation, three named workflows in total
- **Current fully-loaded cost per transaction** for each, calculated with finance, including labour, error-correction time and rework — not just headline salary cost
- **Time saved per process**, estimated conservatively and validated directly with the operational staff who actually performed the work today, rather than assumed from vendor benchmarks
- **A phased investment profile**, tying spend explicitly to delivery milestones rather than requesting the full budget upfront

# The result

The revised business case projected a lower headline return than the original — deliberately so, because every assumption in it had been stress-tested and could be defended under direct questioning. It was approved in a single board meeting, with the CFO specifically noting the credibility of the underlying numbers as the deciding factor.

# The lesson

A business case for automation doesn't need to be the most impressive document in the room. It needs to survive someone asking where a specific number came from. Build the case from finance outward, validate every assumption with the people who'll actually deliver it, and the approval tends to follow.
