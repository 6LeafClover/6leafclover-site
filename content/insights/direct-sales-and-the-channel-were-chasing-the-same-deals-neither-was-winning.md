## The problem

Gallows Pole Technologies had built a capable direct sales team and a growing partner channel, and had never quite got round to deciding how the two should coexist. There was no deal registration process worth the name, no shared visibility into pipeline, and no agreed rule for what happened when a partner and a direct rep found the same prospect.

The result was predictable, once you saw it laid out: prospects receiving two different proposals from the same company, at two different price points, from two teams who didn't know the other existed. Deals slowed while the confusion got sorted out. Partners who lost a deal to direct sales, quietly and without explanation, became partners who stopped bringing new opportunities forward.

## What we did

The fix wasn't a new strategy. It was a rulebook — deliberately unglamorous, deliberately simple:

- A deal registration process with a genuine commercial benefit for the partner who registers first
- Clear territory and segment definitions so both teams knew, in advance, who owned what
- A defined escalation path for the rare cases where a deal is found independently on both sides
- Shared pipeline visibility, so conflict could be caught before the customer ever saw it

None of this required new systems. It required writing down what should have been obvious and holding both teams to it.

## The result

Win rates on previously contested deals rose by roughly a third within two quarters. More significantly, partner satisfaction scores — which had been declining for over a year — began to recover, because partners could see, for the first time, that registering a deal actually meant something.

## The lesson

Channel conflict is rarely a sign of bad faith on either side. It's almost always a sign that nobody wrote the rules down. A short, clear, consistently enforced framework does more to protect partner trust than any amount of relationship management ever will.
