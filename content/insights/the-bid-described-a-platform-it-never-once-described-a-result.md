# The Situation

Copperfield Document Services had strong Objectif Lune delivery credentials and a genuinely capable technical team, but was losing competitive tenders at a rate that didn't match the quality of their actual delivery. A review of several recent losing bids made the pattern clear: each response was a thorough, accurate, comprehensive description of what Objectif Lune could do — and almost entirely silent on what would specifically change for the customer submitting the tender.

# The Rebuild

We rebuilt Copperfield's standard bid response structure around a simple discipline: every section had to open with the customer's problem, in their own operational language, before any platform capability was introduced to address it. Every technical claim required a paired outcome statement — not "the platform supports dynamic content assembly," but "your statements will assemble automatically from live customer data, removing the manual formatting step your team currently spends roughly six hours a week on."

The underlying technical content of the bids barely changed. What changed was the order it appeared in, and the discipline of tying every feature explicitly to a result the evaluator could recognise as relevant to their own organisation.

# Supporting Changes

- A short, mandatory discovery call before any bid response began, specifically to gather the operational detail — hours spent, error rates, current pain points — needed to make outcome statements specific rather than generic
- A reusable library of outcome statements, tagged by industry and use case, so common scenarios didn't need to be rewritten from scratch each time
- A final review stage checking every bid against a simple test: could a non-technical evaluator explain, in one sentence, what would be different for their business after implementation?

# The Result

Win rate on competitive bids rose from roughly one in five to nearly one in three within two quarters, with no change to pricing or underlying technical approach.

# The Lesson

A bid response built around platform capability competes on features, a category where it's easy for a competitor to match or exceed a specific claim. A bid response built around customer outcomes competes on relevance and understanding, which is considerably harder for a competitor to replicate without doing the same discovery work.
