# The Problem

Wiser Time Technologies relied on its partner network for a significant share of quarterly revenue, and depended on partner-submitted forecasts to plan resourcing, cash flow and board reporting. The trouble was that "forecast" meant something different to almost every partner submitting one. Deal stages had no shared definition. A deal marked "committed" by one partner might be a first conversation for another calling the same stage by the same name.

The result, quarter after quarter, was an aggregate number that looked authoritative and was frequently wrong by a wide margin — sometimes optimistic, sometimes pessimistic, always unreliable in ways nobody could diagnose after the fact.

# The Fix

This didn't need a new forecasting platform. It needed a shared language, consistently applied:

- **Common deal-stage definitions**, agreed once and used identically by every partner, with plain-English descriptions of what evidence justified each stage
- **A minimum evidence requirement** — a deal couldn't be marked "committed" without a specific, named next step and date
- **A lightweight weekly update cadence**, designed to take partners minutes rather than hours, so pipeline stayed current without becoming a chore
- **A simple internal dashboard** giving Ashcombe's leadership visibility into pipeline health by partner, not just an aggregate total

# The Result

Forecast accuracy improved from roughly 60% to over 90% within two quarters. Just as significantly, the business gained early warning: when a specific partner's pipeline began thinning, it showed up in week three of the quarter, not on the call at quarter-end when nothing could be done about it.

# The Lesson

Forecasting discipline in a partner network isn't about better software. It's about making sure every partner submitting a number means the same thing by it. Get the definitions right, and the forecast starts telling the truth.
