# The Partner Summit that Changed Nothing

Every year, the same scene plays out.

A company brings its partners together. It books the venue, pays for the keynote and the dinner, and hands out the branded notebooks. On stage, a senior leader says the words everyone expects: "We're here to listen. We're here to learn. We couldn't do this without you."

The partners respond in kind. They speak openly about the things that hold them back: deal registration that doesn't work, margins that don't add up, support that never replies. These conversations are honest, and often uncomfortable. That is exactly why they matter.

Then the event ends. A photograph is taken, with everyone gathered together, smiling. It goes on LinkedIn with a humble post thanking everyone for their deep insight and candid conversations.

And after that, silence.

# The Real Cost

The cost of a summit that goes nowhere isn't just the budget, though that is considerable. It's the trust.

You invited partners to be honest. They were. If nothing changes, they learn a quiet lesson: honesty here is pointless. The next time you ask for feedback, you'll get polite answers instead of true ones. The partners who once pushed you to improve will simply stop pushing.

A summit can only build a relationship if it's the start of something. If it's the whole thing, it's theatre.

# What Good Looks Like

None of this is complicated. It just takes discipline.

## Within One Week

Send every attendee a plain summary of what you heard, in their words, not yours. Don't dress it up.

## Within 30 Days

Pick three things you will fix. Name an owner for each and give each a date.

## Within 90 Days

Tell partners what's done and what isn't. If something can't be fixed, say so and explain why. Partners can live with "not yet" or "no". What they can't live with is silence.

Then, at the next summit, open with a simple slide:

> **You said, we did.**

# The Photo Test

Before you post that group photo, ask yourself a straightforward question. When partners see it, will they think "they listened"? Or will they think "they got their picture"?

The answer isn't decided at the event. It's decided in the weeks that follow.

Saying thank you is easy. Showing it is the hard part, and it's the only part partners believe.
